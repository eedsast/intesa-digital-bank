BIN sponsorship — how the model works

Who can sponsor, how Enfuce and Paynetics operate without owning end-clients, and what an EMI licence lets you do as a pure B2B IBAN sponsor.

Who can be a BIN sponsor?

Two conditions must both be met. First, the provider must hold an EMI or banking licence in the EEA or UK — this makes them the regulated entity of record. Second, they must be a principal member of Visa and/or Mastercard — a separate application to each scheme, with minimum net-asset requirements (typically €5–10M depending on scheme and programme size), proven operational and fraud infrastructure, and minimum volume commitments.


An EMI licence alone does not make you a BIN sponsor. Many EMIs hold the licence but are not scheme principals — they still use Enfuce or GPS as their card sponsor. Getting scheme principal membership takes 12–24 months after EMI licence grant and is a significant additional investment.


Providers in this analysis that can act as BIN sponsor: Enfuce, Paynetics, Wallester, Monavate, B4B Payments, Edenred Payment Solutions, Marqeta (via the 2025 TransactPay acquisition), and RS2 (EMI BaFin/Germany + Visa and Mastercard principal membership via its Beyond by RS2 platform, launched March 2026). BPC and Paymentology cannot — they are technology vendors with no scheme membership of their own.

How Enfuce / Paynetics operate without owning the end-clients

This is the classic BaaS sponsor model under PSD2 Article 19 — the agent model. The fintech does not hold any licence. Enfuce or Paynetics holds the EMI licence and is the regulated entity of record. The fintech is their B2B client and registers as a payment services agent with the national regulator, operating in the name of and under the licence of the sponsor.


IBAN side: The sponsor issues IBANs from their own BIC namespace. The fintech's end-customers get IBANs that are technically accounts at the sponsor, white-labelled under the fintech's brand. The sponsor holds the safeguarding accounts and is responsible for fund segregation under EMD2.


Card side: The sponsor holds a BIN range licensed from Visa/Mastercard. Cards issued to the fintech's end-customers carry that BIN. The sponsor is the issuer of record with the schemes — Visa/Mastercard see the sponsor as the card issuer, not the fintech.


Commercially, the fintech owns the customer relationship — they acquired the customer, run the app/UX, do the branding, and often manage KYC under the sponsor's oversight. But legally and regulatorily, the end-customers are users of the sponsor's regulated service. Interchange from card spend goes to the sponsor as issuer of record; they share a portion back to the fintech under a revenue share agreement.

Can you be an IBAN sponsor without having own end-clients?

Yes — entirely possible and increasingly common. This is the pure BaaS/infrastructure model. Banking Circle, Clear Junction, and Railsr all operate this way.


An EMI licence (under EMD2, transposed in Croatia by HNB) authorises the holder to issue electronic money and provide payment services. It does not require you to serve retail customers directly. You can operate exclusively as a B2B infrastructure layer.


IBAN issuance without own clients: You apply for a BIC/SWIFT code and a national account number range. You issue IBANs from that namespace to your fintech clients' end-customers. You hold the safeguarding accounts at a credit institution. You are responsible for fund segregation — but the deposited funds belong to people you have never spoken to directly.


Practical path for card capability: Apply for EMI licence → operate as IBAN sponsor from day one → partner with an existing scheme principal (e.g. Enfuce or GPS) for card BIN sponsorship while you build volume → apply for scheme principal membership when volume and capital justify it. This mirrors the "Direct scheme participation" proposition — MVP architecture, Phase 3 capability.


Your fintech clients either register as your agents (PSD2 Article 19) or operate under a separate licence of their own (an interbank/interinstitution arrangement rather than an agent model).

Three roles, often confused
How to read this analysis
A card programme needs all three roles filled — by one provider or by several
The roles
Sponsor · Processor · Manager
BIN sponsor holds the licence and scheme membership and carries regulatory liability. Issuer-processor runs authorisation, clearing and settlement. Programme manager owns the customer, the product and the operations.
Why it matters to you
Your platform is the fourth role
The core banking platform is none of these. It holds the ledger and the customer record and must integrate cleanly with whichever combination the customer has already chosen — which means a processor-abstraction layer, not a single hard-wired integration.
💡
Practical consequence: never quote a prospect on the assumption they will take your preferred card partner. Most arrive with an incumbent. Build one internal card API with adapters, certify two providers at launch, and treat the rest as a roadmap. Going direct to scheme membership typically costs 18+ months and substantial frozen collateral, versus roughly 8 weeks through a sponsor — which is why almost every EMI you sell to will be on a sponsored BIN.

Q1 — Licence & BIN sponsorship verdict

Seven of ten providers hold their own EMI or bank licence and will act as BIN sponsor. Only Enfuce, Paynetics and Edenred combine dual-perimeter EEA+UK licences with principal scheme membership — everyone else is single-scheme, single-perimeter, or both. RS2 rounds out the sponsor group with BaFin EMI and dual Visa+MC principal membership via its Beyond by RS2 platform.
Single-scheme sponsorship is the norm, not the exception: Wallester is Visa-only. Any customer wanting both schemes from a single counterparty needs Enfuce, Paynetics, or RS2 — which is itself an argument for your abstraction layer.
The pure technology vendors (BPC and Paymentology) carry no licence and sponsor nothing, which makes them the cleanest complements: no product overlap, no regulatory entanglement, and the customer keeps the scheme relationship. Every customer on them still needs a sponsor, so your adapter must support the split "platform here, sponsorship there" shape.
Marqeta buying TransactPay in 2025 and RS2 launching Beyond by RS2 in 2026 both confirm the direction of travel: processors and platform vendors are acquiring or building EMIs to offer a single contract in Europe. Expect further consolidation and design so that a partner changing shape does not break your customers.

Q2 — Product coverage verdict

Business cards are near-universal; revolving and credit are not. EMI-only sponsors cannot lend, so "credit" at an EMI is usually deferred debit or a partner-funded facility. Genuine revolving credit requires a bank licence or a funding partner behind the programme.
Issuing and acquiring together is rare. Paynetics is the clearest single-counterparty answer for the sponsored EMI segment; RS2 covers the same ground for customers who prefer a platform vendor with its own licence. Everyone else is issuing-only.
Certify two adapters at launch: one licensed sponsor (Enfuce or Paynetics) and one sponsor-agnostic processor or platform (RS2, or Paymentology where the customer already runs it). That pairing covers both the "bring everything" and the "bring your own sponsor" customer. Edenred is the third worth adding early, because its agent model reaches customers who will never hold a licence.
Building sponsor portability in from day one is an architecture requirement, not a nice-to-have: when a sponsor fails or is acquired, the platforms that recover fastest are the ones that can re-point to a second sponsor without rebuilding.
