Payment Processors & IBAN Sponsorship — Competitive Analysis

8 routes to EUR and GBP rails for European EMIs · agency banking, vIBAN providers and direct participation

Q1 — Licence & rail access What licence does the provider hold, and which rails does it reach directly rather than through a further intermediary? Can it hold safeguarding accounts for an EMI?
Q2 — IBAN model Pooled virtual IBANs against a master account, IBANs issued in the end customer's own name, or a sponsored range the EMI controls? The difference drives AML treatment, portability and switching cost.
Three routes to euro rails
How to read this analysis
An EMI needs reachability, settlement and safeguarding — these are the only four ways to get them
The routes
Bank · EMI · NCB · Direct
1. Agency banking through a licensed bank (ClearBank, LHV, Banking Circle). 2. vIBAN provision through another EMI (Clear Junction, OpenPayd, Modulr). 3. Direct participation in T2, TIPS, RT1 or STEP2-T — open to non-bank PSPs since October 2025.
Why this document matters more than it used to
The ladder is now complete
Until late 2025 an EMI's ceiling was an intermediary. That ceiling is gone. A core banking platform can now carry a customer from a pooled vIBAN to direct scheme participation — if the architecture was built for it.
💡
Practical consequence for your platform: do not model "the sponsor" as a configuration value. Model rail access as a pluggable capability with four implementations, and make the internal payment object identical regardless of which one settles it. Everything else in this document is a vendor choice your customer makes; this is an architecture choice you make once.

Q1 — Licence & rail access
Open question — needs a Croatian feasibility check
Since October 2025 non-bank PSPs can be direct participants in T2, TIPS, RT1 and STEP2-T. The Eurosystem policy names both payment institutions and electronic money institutions as eligible — so an EMI licence specifically may not be required. Action: analysis needed on whether this is achievable in Croatia without an EMI licence, e.g. on a lighter HNB payment-institution authorisation.
Q2 — IBAN model
Full scheme participant, if achievable
The institution settles in central bank money for its own account. Sponsorship economics, cut-off windows, risk appetite and insolvency exposure all disappear. Whether we can reach that position in Croatia under something short of an EMI licence is precisely the open question.
Open questions to resolve with HNB — the analysis
1. Does a payment institution authorisation suffice? The Eurosystem policy admits non-bank PSPs generally, and PI capital is €20,000–125,000 against €350,000 for an EMI. The practical test is the service: holding client funds or issuing e-money requires an EMI, whereas merely executing payments is a PI activity. Confirm which side our intended use falls on
2. Has Croatia transposed the SFD and PSD2 amendments? The TARGET Guideline amendment was postponed from April to October 2025 precisely because some member states had not transposed them. Croatia is in the euro area and therefore in T2 and TIPS, but national transposition must be confirmed, not assumed
3. Will HNB grant access to a non-bank PSP, and on what terms? HNB's published payment-institution authorisation pages set out the Article 84/85 process but say nothing about TARGET, TIPS or SEPA access for PIs. This has to be asked directly
4. What are the operational and liquidity conditions? Non-bank PSPs get no intraday credit facility, so every payment is pre-funded from own liquidity; a TARGET account may be used for settlement obligations only, never for safeguarding. Scheme-grade operations — cut-offs, reachability, contingency, business continuity — are a standing obligation
5. What would it cost to run, not just to obtain? Capital, pre-funded liquidity, a permanent compliance and operations function, and audited continuity testing. The barrier here is operational and ongoing, not a one-off application
What holds regardless of the answer
Build the rail abstraction either way. It costs little now and is a rewrite later, and it is what lets a licensed customer migrate from a sponsor to direct participation without re-platforming — a genuine differentiator no competitor markets
If the answer is yes, a PI authorisation suffices: this becomes a strategic option worth costing properly, since it would remove sponsor fees and counterparty risk from any platform-operated environment
If the answer is no, an EMI licence is required: Route 3 is closed to us, and Routes 1 and 2 are the only paths — a licensed bank or an EMI sponsor. Plan on that assumption until the analysis says otherwise
Either way, intraday liquidity management stays a product requirement for customers who do reach direct participation: position monitoring, funding thresholds, alerts, projected end-of-day position
Support dual-running — sponsored and direct in parallel during migration, with no interruption to reachability
💡
Verdict: the most important structural change in European payments infrastructure for this market, and an unresolved question for us specifically. Analysis needed on whether direct participation is achievable in Croatia without an EMI licence — the Eurosystem rules admit payment institutions as well as EMIs, so the answer may be yes on a lighter HNB authorisation, but nothing in HNB's published material confirms it and it must be asked. Until it is answered, plan on Routes 1 and 2 and build the abstraction anyway: it is cheap now, expensive later, and the customer-facing story holds whichever way our own answer lands.

Q1 — Licence & rail access verdict

Only three providers here are licensed banks with direct scheme membership: ClearBank, LHV and Banking Circle. Everything else is an EMI standing between your customer and the scheme, which adds a layer regulators and correspondent banks increasingly scrutinise.
SEPA Direct Debit is the sharpest differentiator. Most providers offer credit transfers; far fewer offer direct debit. Any customer buying your Collections module needs a DD-capable sponsor, which narrows the field substantially.
Dual UK/EU coverage under own licences is rarer than marketing suggests: ClearBank, OpenPayd, Modulr and Paynetics have it; others passport, partner or cover one perimeter only.
Safeguarding is not deposit protection. EMI-route providers safeguard at a credit institution further down the chain, with no deposit guarantee. Your platform should record where client money actually sits, not merely who the contracting provider is.

Q2 — IBAN model verdict and strategy

Named versus pooled is the decision that matters. Named IBANs (OpenPayd, Banking Circle, ClearBank and — confirmed on its own materials — Paynetics) give cleaner reconciliation, better AML treatment and lower switching cost. Pooled vIBANs are cheaper and faster to launch. Make this an explicit attribute in your account model, not an implementation detail.
Route 3 requires our own authorisation and is unresolved: the Eurosystem admits payment institutions as well as EMIs, so analysis is needed on whether direct participation is achievable in Croatia without an EMI licence. Until that is answered, Routes 1 and 2 are our only paths.
Treat sponsorship as a stage, not a state. Pooled vIBAN → named IBAN → own BIC via indirect participation → direct participation in T2/TIPS. Four rungs, one platform, no re-implementation. No core banking vendor in your competitive set markets this.
Shortlist for build: ClearBank and LHV as bank-route adapters — these are the rails we can actually use. Direct participation is an adapter built for licensed customers, not routes we can supply, and own-BIC operation must still work in release one because so many EU EMIs already run that way. Add Clear Junction or OpenPayd when a specific customer requires the EMI route.
