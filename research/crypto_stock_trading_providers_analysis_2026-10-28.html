Two regimes, two licences, and almost nobody holds both

Crypto and listed securities are separate regulatory products in the EU. A provider authorised for one is not authorised for the other, and the contract set differs in each case.

Crypto — MiCA
A CASP authorisation under Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, granted to a named EU entity by one national regulator and passported across the EEA. Separate permissions cover operating a trading platform, executing orders for clients, and custody — a provider may hold some and not others.
Securities — MiFID II
An investment firm authorisation. The permissions that matter here are reception and transmission of orders, execution on behalf of clients, and safekeeping and administration of financial instruments. Portfolio management is a further permission again.
What this means for a single chip
"Investing" in the product catalogue is two procurements, not one. Unless a provider holds both sets, crypto and equities arrive through different counterparties, different custodians and different contracts — behind one screen.
The transitional period is over
MiCA's Article 143(3) grandfathering let firms keep operating under national regimes until 1 July 2026. ESMA confirmed in April 2026 that it would end across the EU on that date, and it did. A provider without a CASP authorisation today is not a procurement option — it is a provider that cannot lawfully serve EEA clients at all.
Authorisation attaches to an entity, not a brand
Every register entry names a legal entity. "Bitpanda" and "Kraken" are brands; the authorised parties are companies such as BP23 CA Limited and Payward Europe Solutions Limited. Confirm in writing which entity signs, where it is established and which permissions its licence actually carries — a group can hold a licence in one subsidiary and contract through another.
Three contracting models — the choice that decides everything else
MODEL A
Introduce
The end customer opens an account with the provider and signs the provider's terms. Intesa Digital refers and is paid a share. Lightest regulatory load, no investment permission needed — but the client relationship, the data and the brand largely leave the bank.
→
MODEL B
Tied agent
Intesa Digital acts under the provider's licence, registered as a tied agent (MiFID II Article 29) in the national register. The provider carries unconditional liability for what the agent does. Fastest route to market with the bank's brand in front.
→
MODEL C
Own permission
Intesa Digital holds its own investment-services permission and CASP authorisation, and buys the provider purely as execution, custody and liquidity. Full control of client, product and economics; the largest capital, compliance and time cost.
💡
Pick the model before picking the provider. Model A and Model C shortlists barely overlap: a pure infrastructure vendor cannot carry an introduced customer, and a consumer exchange will not strip its brand off the journey for free. The EMI licence is relevant to neither — it authorises e-money and payment services and reaches neither crypto nor securities, so one of these three routes is unavoidable for the whole Savings & Investments family.

Securities + crypto, BaFin

Q2 — Contracts
Model B or C — infrastructure, not a brand
Sold as infrastructure to the institution, with end users held at sub-account level under Upvest's custody permission. It offers both "use our licences" and "bring your own" operating models, which maps directly onto Model B and Model C. The public materials do not state who papers the end customer.
Why it leads this route
The only provider found holding both perimeters in one licensed group — securities and crypto, brokerage and custody, under one regulator.
Custody at end-user level, not an omnibus pool, which is what makes a clean exit and a clean client-asset story possible.
Proprietary trading and FX permissions mean execution and currency conversion sit inside the same entity rather than behind a further counterparty.
Reference customers are the comparable set: N26 and Revolut are running embedded investing on it at scale.
Implications & cautions
The public licences page does not itemise a MiCA CASP authorisation. The crypto claim rests on the company's own announcement. Ask for the CASP entity, regulator and permission list in writing before the crypto half of the catalogue depends on it.
Portfolio management is not among the listed permissions — it appears as a use case. If discretionary portfolios are in scope, that is a separate conversation.
Infrastructure vendors do not carry introduced customers. If Model A is the preferred route, Upvest is the wrong shape.
💡
Fit: adapter one, and the only candidate that makes "one investing partner" a real sentence rather than an aspiration. It also fits the architecture already drawn — an adapter behind the Accounts and GL services, with the client relationship staying inside the Intesa app.
Bitpanda B2C + B2B
Exchange with an enterprise arm
Banks in DACH and CEE · RLB NÖ-Wien, LBBW, N26, Plum, iCard, Aircash
Q1 — Licences
CASP confirmed · entity disputed
MiCA CASP authorisations recorded for BP23 CA Limited (MFSA, Malta, 27 January 2025) and Bitpanda GmbH (FMA, Austria, 9 April 2025). Press reporting of the same month instead credits a MiCAR approval from BaFin. The company states it holds 17 licences in total, including PSD2 e-money and MiFID II.
Q2 — Contracts
Co-branded — model not published
Partner language runs from a "joint offering" (RLB NÖ-Wien) to a product "powered by Bitpanda" (Lydia), so the model is negotiated case by case rather than standard. Neither the custody arrangement for partner clients nor the end-customer contracting party is stated publicly.
Strengths
The deepest bank-distribution record in the region: Raiffeisenlandesbank NÖ-Wien and LBBW are live, and the Raiffeisen relationship has been reported as extending across CEE retail.
Six published enterprise products — Investment-as-a-Service, Institutional Custody, Liquidity, Payment, Stablecoin and Tokenize — so liquidity and custody come from the same counterparty as the front end.
A consumer platform covering crypto, stocks, ETFs, metals and more, which is the origin of the "does both" reputation.
CEE-facing and Austrian-rooted, so the commercial and language fit for this programme is unusually close.
Implications & cautions
The dual-asset reputation belongs to the consumer product, not the enterprise one. The published B2B line is crypto and digital assets; stocks and ETFs are not advertised in it. Ask explicitly whether listed securities are available through the enterprise contract, and under which licence.
Sources disagree on which entity and regulator hold the MiCAR authorisation. Get the contracting entity named in the term sheet.
A consumer brand is also a competitor for the same customer. Co-branding that is comfortable on day one looks different once the investing balance matters.
💡
Fit: the strongest commercial fit in the region and a credible adapter two — provided the first meeting resolves the two open questions: does the enterprise product carry listed securities, and which legal entity signs. If securities are out of scope there, Bitpanda becomes a Route 2 provider sitting alongside a separate securities partner.

Q2 — Contracts
Model A or B — proven with a bank
A published "Bitstamp as a Service" offering, and a live retail distribution deal with BBVA giving the bank's own customers bitcoin and ether trading. That is the closest public precedent to what this programme needs. The contracting split is not published.
Strengths
The three permissions that matter — venue, execution and custody — are all inside one authorised entity, so there is no hidden sub-custodian in the chain.
Longest-running EU exchange in the set, now with Robinhood's balance sheet behind it, which answers the counterparty-risk question a bank's third-party risk function will ask first.
A named tier-one bank reference in BBVA, for the exact use case.
Implications & cautions
No equities. The brief assumed Bitstamp covers both asset classes; it does not. A securities partner is required alongside it.
Ownership moved to a US group in 2025. Expect questions on data residency and on where decisions about the EU entity are actually taken.
Derivatives are a different entity under a different regime — do not assume one contract reaches them.
💡
Fit: the strongest crypto-only candidate, on licence clarity and on precedent. The BBVA arrangement is the single most useful reference in this document, because it is a regulated bank distributing a CASP's service to retail customers — the same shape as this programme.

All crypto only — all CASP-authorised
Coinbase Luxembourg S.A. (CSSF, 20 June 2025) · Payward Europe Solutions Ltd and Payward Global Solutions Ltd for Kraken (Central Bank of Ireland, 25 June 2025) · Gemini Intergalactic EU Ltd (MFSA, 21 August 2025) · Foris DAX MT Ltd for Crypto.com and OKX Europe Ltd (MFSA, both 27 January 2025).
Q2 — Contracts
Model A by default
These are consumer platforms first. The realistic arrangement is introduction — the customer leaves for the exchange's own onboarding and signs the exchange's terms. White-labelling a bank's brand over them is possible in principle but is not their published business.
When they make sense
When the objective is to have an answer on crypto quickly without taking balance-sheet, custody or conduct exposure.
Asset breadth and liquidity are far beyond what an infrastructure vendor offers, which matters if the proposition is "everything", not "the top ten".
Three of the five are Malta-authorised; Luxembourg and Ireland cover the rest. All passport across the EEA.
Implications & cautions
The customer leaves the app. Under Model A the relationship, the data and the balance sit with the exchange, and the referral fee is all that comes back.
These are direct competitors for the primary relationship, not neutral suppliers.
MFSA's speed in authorising several large platforms has itself drawn scrutiny. That is a reputational input to the choice, not a legal one.
💡
Fit: a fallback, not a strategy. If the MiCA-regulated crypto chip exists to say the bank has crypto, Model A with one of these delivers it in weeks. If it exists to hold balances, it does not.

What actually gets signed

The same documents recur whichever provider is chosen. Under Model A most of them move to the end customer; under Models B and C they are Intesa Digital's to negotiate.

Intesa Digital signs
Master services agreement with the schedule of permissions actually relied on.
Execution and custody terms — who holds the assets, segregation, whether sub-custodians are used and who they are.
Outsourcing agreement meeting the EBA outsourcing guidelines, with audit and regulator access rights.
DORA register entry and exit plan — a provider of this kind is an ICT third party, and a documented exit strategy is not optional.
Data processing agreement and the AML responsibility split, including the crypto transfer-of-funds (travel rule) obligations.
The end customer signs
Model A — the provider's own account terms, risk disclosures and privacy notice. Intesa Digital's name appears only on the referral.
Models B and C — Intesa Digital's terms of business, order execution policy, conflicts policy, cost and charges disclosure, and the risk warnings for each asset class. For crypto, the MiCA white paper and marketing-communication rules apply to whoever distributes.
Easy to miss
Appropriateness, not just suitability. Execution-only investing still requires an appropriateness assessment, and a knowledge test is a product requirement, not a compliance afterthought.
Product governance. A target market must be defined and reviewed for every instrument distributed.
Client-asset reporting and the annual statement obligations that come with holding assets for retail clients.
Tax reporting — DAC8 extends automatic exchange to crypto-asset reporting, and the operational load lands on whoever faces the client.

Verdict

The premise needs correcting. Bitstamp is crypto only, and Bitpanda's published enterprise line is crypto-led — the stocks and ETFs belong to its consumer product. The provider that genuinely covers both perimeters in one licensed group is Upvest.
Plan for two counterparties, be pleased if it is one. Budget, integrate and paper the investing proposition as two procurements; collapsing them later is easy, discovering the gap after signing is not.
Choose the contracting model first. Model A ships in weeks and keeps nothing; Model C keeps everything and costs a licence. Model B — tied agent under a provider's authorisation — is the usual answer for a bank-branded journey.
Shortlist: Upvest for both asset classes; Bitstamp for crypto if a separate securities partner is taken; Bitpanda if the regional distribution relationship outweighs the two open questions.
Grandfathering ended on 1 July 2026. Any provider still trading on a national regime has no lawful basis in the EEA. Check the entity in the register before the first meeting, not after.
The EMI licence reaches none of this. Crypto needs a CASP authorisation and securities a MiFID II permission — directly or through a provider's. That constraint covers the whole Savings & Investments family, not just this chip.
