How to read it

What already runs, what Phase I builds, and what waits.

Lending and decisioning already run

The loan management system, the decision engine and the loans API set are built and in service. They carry the instalment products, the scoring, the limits and the pricing — which is why they were never candidates for a bought module.

Phase I is the rest of the bank

Clients, accounts and deposits, general ledger, reference data and fees, plus marketplace and loyalty, the four front ends, the orchestration layer and the adapters to the payment processor, card processor, core banking system and signing provider. It is a large phase because it is everything except the lending that already exists.

One adapter per outside system

Payments, cards, core banking and digital signature each sit behind their own integration service, and services talk over REST where a synchronous answer is needed and over Kafka or RabbitMQ where it is not. That is what lets a provider be swapped, and what makes the phase II modules additive rather than invasive.

Phase II is reach, not foundation

FX, trade finance and stock trading adapters, and notifications through a provider such as Infobip. None of them is needed for the bank to open; each one extends what it can offer once it has.

Phase approach

A complete core first, extensions after it is live.

Phase I is not a pilot. Every module in it goes live together and gives a working bank end to end: infrastructure, accounts and ledger, lending, payments, cards, onboarding, signing and notifications. Phase II adds the optional capabilities once the core is stable.
